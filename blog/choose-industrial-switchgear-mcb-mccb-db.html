How to Choose Industrial Switchgear: MCB, MCCB & DB

A project-ready selection sequence for final circuits, feeders and distribution boards — written for India B2B buyers.

Choosing industrial switchgear is not about picking the cheapest MCB on a dealer list. For commercial buildings, factories and institutional projects across India — including Ranchi, Jharkhand and Bihar — the device must match the load, the fault level, the panel layout and the inspection expectations on site. This guide walks contractors, retailers supporting project sales, and facility buyers through a practical selection sequence for MCB, MCCB and distribution boards (DB).

Start with the single-line diagram (SLD), not the catalogue

Before you open a price list, confirm what the electrical design actually requires. An SLD (or at least a DB schedule) tells you incomers, sub-mains, final circuits, and where earth leakage or residual current devices sit. If you only have a handwritten load list, convert it into circuits: lighting, power, HVAC, machinery auxiliaries, UPS bypass, and spare ways.

Ask three clarifying questions early:

  • What is the system voltage and phasing (single-phase final circuits vs three-phase feeders)?
  • What breaking capacity (kA) has the consultant assumed at that board?
  • Is this a new panel, a retrofit into an existing DB, or a like-for-like replacement?

Skipping these questions is how projects end up with devices that trip under normal inrush, or worse, devices that cannot clear a fault safely.

MCB: final-circuit protection done right

Miniature Circuit Breakers protect final circuits. Selection is mainly about rated current (In), poles, trip curve, and breaking capacity appropriate to the board.

Rated current and diversity

Size the MCB to the circuit design current and the cable it protects — not to “whatever the machine nameplate says” alone. Cable size and MCB rating must stay coordinated. If a consultant has already frozen cable sizes, your MCB choice is constrained by that design.

Trip curves (B / C / D in common India practise)

Curve choice affects nuisance tripping. Lighting and general socket circuits often use curves suited to modest inrush. Circuits with motors, transformers or LED driver banks may need a curve that tolerates higher starting current — but only within what the design allows. Do not “upgrade the curve” casually to stop trips; investigate inrush and earth faults first.

Poles and neutrals

Match poles to the circuit. Mixing single-pole devices on circuits that need simultaneous disconnection of all live conductors creates unsafe isolation habits during maintenance. Follow the panel schedule.

MCCB: feeders and higher fault levels

Moulded Case Circuit Breakers typically sit on incomers and larger feeders where continuous current and fault level exceed practical MCB ranges. Key fields on any MCCB enquiry:

  • Frame size and rated current (fixed or adjustable thermal/magnetic, depending on product family)
  • Breaking capacity at the relevant voltage
  • Utilisation category and number of poles
  • Accessories: shunt trip, under-voltage release, rotary handle, spreaders — only if the panel design needs them

For replacement jobs in running plants, photograph the nameplate and note the panel maker. “Almost same rating” is not the same as dimensional and terminal compatibility.

Distribution boards: the housing matters as much as the breakers

A DB is not a decorative metal box. It must accommodate the incomer, outgoing ways, neutral and earth bars, cable entry, heat dissipation and future spare ways. Common procurement mistakes:

  • Ordering a DB with too few ways “to save cost,” then overcrowding it later
  • Ignoring IP / environment for dusty sheds or outdoor kiosks
  • Forgetting knockouts, gland plates or form of separation expected by the panel builder

If you are buying a ready DB plus MCBs as a package, insist that the schedule (circuit labels) is printed or provided so commissioning teams do not guess.

Coordination and selectivity (keep it simple on site)

Selectivity means a fault on a final circuit should clear on that MCB without taking down the MCCB incomer whenever practical. Perfect selectivity is a design topic; as a buyer you can still help by:

  • Not substituting higher-rated outgoing devices without consultant approval
  • Keeping manufacturer families consistent inside one board when the design assumed that
  • Recording settings on adjustable MCCBs after commissioning

Documentation buyers should request

For industrial and institutional work, ask for datasheets matching the exact catalogue reference, and keep packing lists against your BOM. GeM pathway buyers should share the catalogue identifiers used in their bid early so the supply conversation stays aligned.

What to put in your RFQ / supplier brief

A clear RFQ (request for quotation) or supplier brief cuts clarification loops. Copy this checklist into WhatsApp or email when you contact ATRICL — then link the full line list as a photo or spreadsheet.

  • Project / site identity: site name, city (Ranchi or other Jharkhand / Bihar location), and whether work is greenfield, retrofit or breakdown replacement.
  • Board / panel context: incomer size if known, photo of existing DB or MCCB nameplate, and whether a consultant SLD or DB schedule exists.
  • MCB line list: poles, rated current (In), trip curve preference if specified, quantity, and any residual-current / RCBO notes from the design.
  • MCCB line list: frame / In, poles, breaking capacity (kA) if stated, accessories (shunt trip, rotary handle), and whether adjustable settings are required.
  • Distribution board: ways / size, IP or environment notes (dusty shed, outdoor kiosk), cable entry expectations, and spare ways needed.
  • Brand / make notes: consultant-approved list if any; otherwise preferred families. ATRICL will answer honestly about what we can supply — including authorised brands we partner with.
  • Documentation: datasheet needs, packing-list expectations, GeM catalogue reference if the order is institutional / GeM-linked.
  • Logistics: delivery gate, preferred date, unloading constraints, and who receives material on site.
  • Commercial path: private commercial PO vs GeM pathway; quote validity window you need.

FAQ

Can I replace an MCB with a higher rating if it keeps tripping?

Not without checking cable size and the reason for tripping. Upsizing protection without upsizing the conductor is unsafe. Find the load, loose terminations, or earth fault first.

Is MCCB always better than MCB?

No. They serve different ranges. Use what the design specifies. Over-specifying everything as MCCB raises cost and may not fit the board.

Do I need the same brand across the whole panel?

Consistency helps with accessories, look/feel and sometimes selectivity literature. Follow consultant preference when stated; otherwise ask before mixing frames.

What should Ranchi / Jharkhand project buyers emphasise?

Clear delivery location, monsoon-season storage for outdoor boards, and photos of dusty or high-ambient sites so enclosure IP is not underspecified.

Working with panel builders

When a panel builder owns the GA drawing, align your purchase list to their approved makes list. Substitutions mid-build cause terminal mismatch and delayed FATs. If ATRICL is quoting components for a builder, share the builder’s contact so clarification is direct.

For in-house maintenance teams assembling small DBs, still follow IS/project practise for clearances, neutral bars and cable bending space — compact boards that cannot be terminated safely are not savings.
