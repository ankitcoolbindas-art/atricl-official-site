Major Electrical Brands in India — Market Overview for Buyers | ATRICL

A research-informed orientation to common preferred-make and market lists — for buyers who still need to verify the project brief, availability and authorisation.

Important authorisation disclaimer

This article is an educational overview of brands commonly specified or sold in the Indian electrical market. Naming a brand does not mean ATRICL is an authorised partner, dealer, or stockist of that brand. ATRICL’s owner-confirmed authorised brands are only C&S Electric, Khul BLDC fan, Greatwhite, Ledure Lighting Ltd, and Jiva — see the authorised brands page and catalog downloads.

Electrical buyers in India encounter long preferred-make lists, consultant schedules, tender documents and dealer inventories. The names below are a practical market overview, not a ranking, recommendation or exhaustive directory. A brand’s appearance in a public list should be treated as a prompt to check the current project specification, the exact product family and the supply route.

1. Switchgear / MCB / MCCB / DB

These names commonly appear across project preferred-make lists and Indian distribution channels for low-voltage protection, control and distribution equipment. Exact approval is always project-specific.

  • Schneider Electric: commonly specified for low-voltage protection, control and distribution systems.
  • Legrand: widely distributed for distribution boards, protection and wiring-accessory ranges.
  • Siemens: appears in industrial and infrastructure switchgear and automation specifications.
  • ABB: commonly referenced for low-voltage protection, control and industrial distribution.
  • Lauritz Knudsen (L&T Electrical & Automation): the current brand identity associated with L&T’s electrical and automation portfolio.
  • Havells: widely distributed across residential, commercial and light-industrial electrical categories.
  • Hager: appears in protection, distribution-board and modular electrical specifications.
  • C&S Electric: appears in switchgear and electrical project lists; it is also an ATRICL owner-confirmed authorised brand.
  • Polycab: commonly encountered across cables and broader electrical distribution categories.
  • Eaton: appears in industrial and commercial power-management and protection specifications.
  • Anchor by Panasonic: commonly distributed in wiring accessories and related electrical categories.

2. Cables & wires

For cables, the conductor, insulation, voltage class, construction, installation method and test requirements matter more than a name alone. Public preferred-make examples commonly include:

  • Polycab: widely distributed across building wires, power cables and related electrical products.
  • Havells: commonly listed for building wires and low-voltage cable categories.
  • Finolex: commonly encountered in wire and cable procurement lists.
  • KEI: appears across LV, power and infrastructure cable requirements.
  • Grandlay: appears in public institutional preferred-make lists for cable categories.
  • Gloster: appears in public preferred-make lists for wires and cables.

3. Lighting

Lighting choices should follow the lux calculation, mounting height, environment, glare requirement, controls and maintenance plan—not just a familiar retail name.

  • Signify / Philips: commonly distributed across professional and general lighting categories.
  • Wipro Lighting: appears in commercial and institutional lighting discussions.
  • Havells: broadly distributed across consumer, commercial and project lighting.
  • Crompton: commonly encountered in lamps, luminaires and electrical consumer categories.
  • Bajaj: distributed across general lighting and electrical consumer ranges.
  • Syska: commonly encountered in consumer and general LED lighting channels.

4. Fans / FMEG

Fan selection should check airflow, room geometry, duty cycle, controls, noise expectations and service access. Commonly distributed or specified names include:

  • Havells: a broadly distributed FMEG and fan brand in India.
  • Crompton: commonly encountered in residential and commercial fan categories.
  • Orient: widely distributed in fans and related consumer electrical products.
  • Usha: commonly encountered in fan and small-appliance channels.
  • Atomberg: appears in modern BLDC-fan discussions and consumer distribution.
  • Khul: an ATRICL owner-confirmed authorised brand; confirm the required model and availability against the RFQ.

5. Modular switches

For modular switches and sockets, check the series, plate system, accessory compatibility, installation environment and project-approved make. Names commonly listed include:

  • Legrand: commonly listed across modular wiring-accessory families.
  • Schneider: appears in modular switch and wiring-accessory specifications.
  • Havells: broadly distributed in modular accessories and consumer electrical categories.
  • ABB: appears in project and industrial electrical accessory schedules.
  • MK / Honeywell: commonly listed in institutional and commercial wiring-accessory contexts.
  • Crabtree: appears in modular switch and socket market lists.

6. How buyers should use preferred-make lists

A preferred-make list is not a substitute for a specification. Treat it as a controlled shortlist and ask the consultant, owner or tender document what is actually acceptable for the package.

  • Copy the exact item description, rating, poles, breaking capacity, IP requirement and quantity from the BOQ or SLD.
  • Separate “approved makes” from “or equivalent” language; do not assume the latter gives automatic substitution permission.
  • Ask for the exact series and catalogue reference, not only the parent brand.
  • Record whether the project needs datasheets, test documents, warranty terms or a particular delivery lot.
  • For a practical line-list template, see the switchgear RFQ checklist, then send the completed RFQ through the Request a Quote page.
