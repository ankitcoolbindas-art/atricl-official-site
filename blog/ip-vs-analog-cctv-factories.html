IP vs Analog CCTV for Factories

Choose technology from site zones and expansion plans, not megapixel marketing.

Factories comparing IP and analog CCTV are usually trying to answer three questions: Will we see enough detail? Can we expand later? What will cabling and recording actually cost to run? This guide is for plant engineers, security supervisors and electrical contractors supporting industrial sites in India — including facilities around Ranchi, Jharkhand and Bihar — who need a practical decision frame without vendor hype.

Define the job before choosing the technology

List zones: main gate, weighbridge, material yard, shop floor, stores, perimeter, parking, boiler / utility areas. For each zone note day/night need, expected viewing distance, and whether you need identification (face/number plate-ish detail) or mere detection (someone is present). Many plants overspend on camera megapixels at the gate and underspend on coverage at blind corners.

Analog (including HD-over-coax families) — where it still fits

“Analog” in modern tenders often means coax-based HD camera families that reuse older cabling patterns, not only legacy SD cameras. Strengths:

  • Familiar installation craft for many local technicians
  • Potentially simpler mental model: camera → DVR
  • Useful when upgrading cameras on existing coax runs in stages

Limits to plan for:

  • Feature velocity and analytics options are typically stronger on IP platforms
  • Long-term expansion toward enterprise VMS / multi-site viewing may be harder
  • Cable quality and jointing still make or break picture quality

IP cameras — strengths and responsibilities

IP cameras send video over Ethernet networks (often with PoE). Strengths:

  • Flexible resolutions and lens options for gates vs wide yards
  • Easier path to remote viewing when the plant network and policies allow it
  • Better fit when you already have structured cabling standards

Responsibilities you must accept:

  • Switch capacity, PoE budget, and VLAN / security hygiene
  • Cyber basics: change defaults, segment cameras from office PCs when possible
  • Storage planning on NVR / servers (see our NVR article)

Hybrid realities on Indian factory floors

Many sites run hybrid for years: IP at the gate and new sheds, analog/HD coax in older halls. That can be rational if the recorder and viewing workflow are understood by the security team. Problems appear when nobody documents channel maps, passwords live on a sticky note, and the only person who understands the DVR leaves the company.

Decision matrix (simplified)

  • Choose IP-first for new plants, multi-building campuses, and when remote multi-user viewing is a stated requirement.
  • Consider coax HD refresh when existing cabling is healthy and budget is staged year-by-year.
  • Do not decide on megapixels alone — lens, mounting height, lighting and WDR behaviour at gates matter more.

Power, earthing and surge — the electrical contractor’s role

CCTV fails as often from power and earthing issues as from “bad cameras.” Outdoor poles need thoughtful earthing and surge protection practise appropriate to the site. Coordinate camera power with the electrical team; do not treat CCTV as an isolated low-voltage hobby on an industrial HT/LT campus.

FAQ

Is IP always clearer than analog?

Not automatically. Mounting, lens, lighting and compression settings dominate. A well-installed HD coax camera can outperform a poorly aimed IP camera.

Do I need fiber?

Only for longer backbone distances or electrically noisy routes where copper Ethernet is impractical. Many plants run fine on copper within buildings plus careful uplinks.

Can we keep old cameras?

Sometimes during phased upgrades. Test picture quality and spare parts reality before promising management a full reuse.

Total cost beyond camera price

Compare cabling labour, switchgear for power, disk capacity, training time for guards, and spare camera inventory. A cheaper camera that needs constant realignment is expensive. Standardise mounts and junction boxes where possible so maintenance is repeatable across gates and sheds.

Cyber hygiene minimums for IP systems

  • Change default passwords before going live
  • Keep recorder firmware update policy agreed with IT
  • Limit internet exposure; prefer VPN for remote viewing when IT requires it
  • Document who holds admin credentials

Electrical contractors delivering cameras should coordinate with plant IT rather than improvising public port forwards.

Installation craft differences

Analog/HD-coax installers focus on BNC quality, correct impedance cable and clean power at the DVR. IP installers must additionally terminate RJ45 correctly, certify longer runs when required, and label switch ports. Cross-training your electrical team on both crafts reduces dependency on a single subcontractor.

For factories near Ranchi and across Jharkhand or Bihar, skilled IP labour availability varies by district — factor training time into project schedules rather than assuming day-one perfection.

Viewing experience for security staff

A system that only the vendor can navigate will be abandoned on night shifts. Prefer recorders with clear camera trees, simple search by time, and monitors placed where guards actually sit. Multi-monitor layouts help gates; a single cluttered grid does not.

Agree on a naming convention: Gate-IN, Gate-OUT, Yard-North, Store-1 — not Camera01…Camera32 with no map.
